\section{Research Background}

\subsection*{Overview}

Maritime shipping plays a crucial role in global trade, facilitating around 80\% of all commerce \cite{owid-co2-emissions-from-transport}. This makes it essential for globalization, economic development, and maintaining living standards. However, the sector also contributes significantly to global greenhouse gas emissions, accounting for 2-3\% of global emissions and approximately 10\% of all transport-related emissions \cite{owid-ghg-emissions-by-sector, owid-co2-emissions-from-transport}.

In response, the International Maritime Organization (IMO) has set ambitious targets to curb emissions from international shipping. By 2030, the IMO aims to reduce annual greenhouse gas emissions by 20-30\% compared to 2008 levels, with a more aggressive target of 70-80\% reduction by 2040 \cite{imo-target}.

\subsection{Preliminary Analysis of Electro-Fuels Options}

\subsubsection*{Overview}

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{./images/Falling Solar Prices.png}
  \caption{Falling cost of solar electricity vs Fossil Fuels \cite{owid-cheap-renewables-growth}}
  \label{fig:solar-vs-fossil-fuels}
\end{figure}

Recent years have witnessed a dramatic decline in the cost of low-carbon electricity sources, particularly solar and wind power, as illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:solar-vs-fossil-fuels}. This cost reduction has significantly enhanced the viability of transitioning to a low-carbon economy, yet substantial challenges persist in the so-called hard-to-abate sectors, among which marine shipping features prominently.

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.9\linewidth]{./images/lithium-battery-prices-fall-bnef.jpg}
  \caption{Falling cost of lithium ion battery packs \cite{bnef-lithium-battery-price-fall}}
  \label{fig:falling-lithium-battery-prices}
\end{figure}

The predominant method for storing electrical energy remains electrochemical batteries, with lithium-ion technology leading the charge. These batteries have increasingly displaced fossil fuels in passenger vehicles and, more recently, have begun penetrating short and medium-distance trucking applications \cite{bnef-clean-trucks-china}. This transformation has been driven largely by the precipitous decline in lithium battery prices, as demonstrated in Figure \ref{fig:falling-lithium-battery-prices}. The technology's reach has even extended to inland shipping routes, where standardized and swappable battery packs are being deployed in China \cite{mb-china-battery-swapping-ships}. Major battery manufacturers such as CATL are making substantial investments in these markets, signaling confidence in the technology's continued expansion \cite{Maersk-CATL-partnerships}.

However, despite remarkable advances in both price and energy density, lithium-ion batteries remain largely impractical for deep-sea shipping, which accounts for approximately 70\% of marine shipping emissions \cite{share-of-different-range-ships}. The fundamental constraint lies in the current energy density limitations of lithium-ion technology, which ranges from 0.1 to 0.5 kWh/kg \cite{battery-density, battey-density-autos}—substantially lower than gasoline, which can exceed 10 kWh/kg \cite{energy-densitie-JMB}. This considerable disparity in energy density presents a formidable barrier to the electrification of long-distance maritime transport.

In response to the energy density limitations of lithium-ion batteries, researchers and policymakers have increasingly explored the use of abundant and inexpensive solar electricity to produce alternative fuels, primarily through electrolysis. This approach has given rise to a diverse landscape of electro-fuel options, which can be systematically organized into four broad classifications:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Simple electro-fuels} - hydrogen and ammonia, which represent the most straightforward products of electrolytic processes
    
    \begin{figure}[h!]
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{./images/synthetic-hydrocarbons-process-diagram.jpg}
		\caption{High Level Process Diagram for synthetic hydrocarbon production from renewable electricity \cite{synthetic-hydrocarbon-process-diagram}}
		\label{fig:synthetic-hydrocarbon-process-diagram}
	\end{figure}
    
    \item \textbf{Synthetic hydrocarbons} - primarily methane and methanol, whose production pathways are illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:synthetic-hydrocarbon-process-diagram}, which depicts the high-level process for synthesizing these fuels from low-carbon electricity
    
    \begin{figure}[h!]
		\centering
		\includegraphics[width=\linewidth]{./images/idea of metal powder electrofuels.jpg}
		\caption{Metal based electro-fuels as secondary energy carriers \cite{metal-fuels-JMB}}
		\label{fig:metal-electro-fuels-diagram}
	\end{figure}
    
    \item \textbf{Metal-based electro-fuels} - including iron, aluminium, magnesium, silicon, and boron. Figure \ref{fig:metal-electro-fuels-diagram} demonstrates the high-level process by which these metal-based electro-fuels can function as recyclable secondary energy carriers, offering a distinctive closed-loop energy storage paradigm
    
    \item \textbf{Metal hydrides (hydrogen carriers)} - such as MgH$_2$, AlH$_3$, LiBH$_4$, and NaAlH$_4$, providing an alternative means of storing and transporting hydrogen in a more dense and stable form than its gaseous state
\end{itemize}

In the following subsections, a preliminary analysis is conducted to compare the mass energy and volumetric energy densities of these various electro-fuel options in order to understand their viability for marine shipping applications.

Figure \ref{fig:simplified_fuel_energy_density} presents the fuel energy density calculations for the various electro-fuel options currently under investigation for marine applications. Heavy Fuel Oil (HFO) serves as the reference fuel in these comparisons, as it remains the dominant fuel source for global marine shipping due to its low cost and widespread availability. 

Hydrogen, which is being explored as a potential solution for several hard-to-decarbonise sectors including shipping and aviation, requires pressurisation at approximately 500 bar. This compression requirement drastically diminishes the fuel's energy density, presenting significant challenges for practical implementation. Even methanol, which is considered a relatively straightforward drop-in replacement for marine shipping and has garnered substantial support from major shipping companies such as Maersk \cite{methanol-ships}, exhibits significantly lower mass and volumetric energy densities compared to HFO.

\begin{figure}[h!]
\centering
  \includegraphics[width=0.45\linewidth]{./images/metal fuels bunkering procedure.jpg}
  \caption{Proposed bunkering procedure for metal fuels (iron powder in this specific case) \cite{iron-powder-container-ships}}
  \label{fig:metal-fuels-bunkering-procedure}
\end{figure}

As illustrated in Figure \ref{fig:simplified_fuel_energy_density}, metal fuels demonstrate considerable promise in terms of both mass and volumetric energy densities, despite the relatively limited research effort devoted to their development. All metal fuels examined display higher volumetric energy densities than HFO, with boron demonstrating superior performance across all available electro-fuel options and surpassing even HFO itself. However, metal fuels present numerous complications that must be addressed for practical deployment. To make metal fuels economically viable, the metal oxide by-products generated during combustion cannot simply be discharged into the ocean. Instead, these by-products must be retained onboard the vessel and offloaded at ports for subsequent recycling using renewable energy sources, as shown in Fig \ref{fig:metal-electro-fuels-diagram}. The bunkering procedure for a metal fuels system is depicted in Figure \ref{fig:metal-fuels-bunkering-procedure}. It is crucial to recognise that energy density calculations which fail to account for the mass and volume of the metal oxide by-products can be highly misleading and do not reflect the true practical performance of these fuel systems.
